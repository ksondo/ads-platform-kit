Africa Digital Synergy | Digital Tontine / Njangi & Tontine-to-Business Platform
1. What the Platform Is
The Digital Tontine / Njangi platform is Africa Digital Synergy's attempt to take a financial practice that is 
already deeply understood in African communities and give it the digital infrastructure needed for 
transparency, scale, financial inclusion and productive use of savings.
The project goes beyond converting a paper contribution book into an app. Its broader direction is 
Tontine-to-Business: use disciplined community savings and verified contribution history as a pathway 
toward personal goals, enterprise creation, financing readiness and long-term financial participation.
The larger journey
Save together -> build verified history -> reach a goal -> identify a productive opportunity -> close the 
funding gap -> launch or invest -> create new income and transaction history.
2. The Problem the Platform Addresses
Tontines and savings groups work because they combine trust, social discipline and regular contribution.
But many groups still depend on cash, handwritten records, spreadsheets or messaging groups. This 
makes disputes, reconciliation, continuity, transparency and scale difficult.
 Members may not have a real-time view of contributions, arrears, payouts or group balances.
 Officials carry heavy manual reconciliation responsibilities and can become single points of failure.
 Small but consistent savings histories remain invisible to banks and other formal financial partners.
 A member may complete several tontine cycles without converting that discipline into a structured 
savings goal or productive asset.
 Community groups can struggle to connect safely to mobile money, banks, microfinance, insurance, 
investment or entrepreneurship programmes.
 Traditional systems are often local and relationship-dependent, limiting their ability to scale across 
workplaces, associations, cooperatives or diaspora-linked groups.
3. Flexible Scheme Architecture
The platform should not assume that every tontine operates the same way. Groups need configurable 
rules while the system preserves a complete audit trail.

5. Tontine-to-Business
The strategic extension is to help a member turn savings discipline into productive capital. Instead of 
asking only, 'Whose turn is it to receive the pot?', the platform can also ask, 'What are you trying to build 
with this money?'
A member may choose a business pathway such as tailoring, poultry, small retail, food processing, 
agriculture, transport services or another approved opportunity. The platform can then connect the 
savings plan to the real startup requirement.
1. The member selects or defines a business objective.

2. The platform presents the approved startup requirements and indicative capital need for that 
business pathway.
3. A contribution plan is configured around the member's capacity and desired launch period.
4. Savings progress is tracked against the business target.
5. If a funding gap remains, eligible partner financing or support programmes may be identified.
6. Before launch, the member can complete business-readiness activities such as planning, budgeting, 
training or documentation.
7. Funds are released or financing is activated according to the rules of the scheme and regulated 
partner.
8. The new business can then connect to Africa Digital Synergy's digital tools, creating transaction 
history and a new cycle of financial visibility.
Why this changes the business
A normal digital tontine digitizes savings. Tontine-to-Business can turn the platform into an 
entrepreneurship and financial-inclusion engine, connecting community discipline to enterprise 
creation.
6. The Intelligent Financial Guidance Layer
The intelligent layer is intended to help people understand and plan; it must not be allowed to invent 
balances, interest, payout eligibility or regulated financial decisions. Those remain controlled by the 
platform's rules and licensed partners.
 Scheme guidance: explain the selected tontine's rules, obligations, payout sequence, penalties and 
member rights from the actual configured policy.
 Affordability support: use member-entered income and expense information to help explore 
sustainable contribution levels.
 Goal planning: compare how long a target may take under different contribution amounts and 
frequencies using deterministic calculations.
 Business readiness: connect the member's savings target to approved business knowledge, startup 
requirements and practical preparation.
 Financial education: provide grounded learning on budgeting, saving, borrowing, risk, cash flow, 
responsible credit and entrepreneurship.
 Exception explanation: help members understand arrears, missed contributions, penalties, 
documentation requirements or payout conditions without changing the underlying rules.
 Opportunity matching: surface approved financing, training, government, bank, MFI, insurance or 
entrepreneurship programmes relevant to the member's objective.
 Group intelligence: authorized administrators can receive trend explanations around participation, 
arrears or scheme performance while avoiding inappropriate profiling.

8. Financial Partner and Regulatory Model
Africa Digital Synergy should position the platform as technology, rules, reconciliation, user experience 
and intelligence infrastructure - not as an unlicensed deposit-taking institution.
 Actual holding, transfer, lending or investment of customer funds should occur through 
appropriately regulated banks, microfinance institutions, mobile-money operators or payment 
providers.
 The platform can integrate partner APIs, display balances and transaction status, maintain scheme 
ledgers and reconcile expected versus confirmed settlement.
 Credit, investment and return-bearing products should only be activated through structures 
permitted by the applicable jurisdiction and partner license.
 KYC, AML, consumer-protection, data-privacy, dispute and audit requirements should be configurable
by market and partner.
 The user should always be able to see the difference between group rules, platform fees and 
regulated-partner charges.
9. Revenue and Commercial Model
The platform can support multiple commercial models, but fees should remain transparent and 
configurable rather than buried in code.
 Individual, group or organization subscriptions.
 Scheme-administration or premium governance packages.
 Implementation and digitisation services for associations, cooperatives, employers or community 
organizations.
 Transaction-service revenue where permitted by the payment/financial-partner agreement.
 Business-readiness and launch-support packages under the Tontine-to-Business model.
 Financial-education programmes for institutions or development partners.
 Referral or distribution income from banks, MFIs, insurers or other partners where legally permitted.
 Future enterprise tools for cooperatives, employee savings schemes and large associations.
Earlier platform discussions also contemplated configurable percentage-based company participation or 
return mechanisms. Those options should remain administrative configuration subject to transparent 
disclosure, product economics and regulatory review - not a fixed assumption applied to every scheme.

11. Why the Opportunity Can Scale
The addressable market is not just existing formal tontines. It includes people who can save only very 
small amounts, informal groups that want better transparency, organizations that want structured 
member finance, financial institutions seeking new customers, and entrepreneurs who need a disciplined
path from savings to startup capital.
As transaction and contribution history accumulates, members can become more visible to formal 
finance. That history should not automatically become a credit score, but with consent and appropriate 
models it can provide licensed partners with stronger evidence than an undocumented cash economy.
12. Initial Rollout Path
1. Start with a controlled digital tontine: group creation, members, configurable rules, contributions, 
ledger, reconciliation, statements and payouts.
2. Integrate at least one regulated payment/financial partner for settlement while preserving clear 
platform-versus-custodian responsibilities.
3. Pilot low-value schemes and normal rotating groups to prove trust, reconciliation and administrator 
usability.
4. Add the intelligent guidance layer for scheme explanation, financial education and deterministic goal 
simulations.
5. Introduce Tontine-to-Business for a small number of approved business pathways with defined startup
requirements.
6. Connect eligible members to training, financing or entrepreneurship partners and measure whether 
savings progress translates into productive business launch.
7. Expand into cooperatives, employer groups, associations and multi-country structures only after 
regulatory and operational validation.
